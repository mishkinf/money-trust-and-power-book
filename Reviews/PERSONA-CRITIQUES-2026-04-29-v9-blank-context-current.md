## Reviewer 1 — Big 5 Trade Nonfiction Acquisitions Editor

**Native verdict:** acquire with revisions.

**Memo summary:** The book has a real argument, strong timing, memorable human stories, and a distinctive editorial frame. The strongest hook is: money is not a thing but a promise backed by institutions, and every monetary regime decides who pays when the promise fails. The likely reader is the serious policy-curious generalist: readers of Tooze, Ahamed, Graeber, Tett, Wolf, Kelton, *Lords of Finance*, *Crashed*, and the better end of economics/Foreign Affairs/Odd Lots culture.

**Main concerns:** The structure is more episodic than protagonist-driven. Backwell and Attwood land well; Keynes lands strongly; Volcker arrives late as a full protagonist. Chapters 1-5 sometimes over-explain the institutional-credit lesson. Chapter 12 is the main structural problem because it carries 2008, Japan, COVID inflation, Volcker, Latin America, and crypto in one final chapter.

4. **Make policy payoff more concrete without becoming a manifesto.** Named by heterodox, lay reader, and historian. The specific heterodox ask was Job Guarantee / employment-buffer-stock; this has now been added concisely to the Epilogue.

5. **Correct/nuance early-origin claims.** Named by historian; relevant to credibility. The Song *jiaozi* passage has now been corrected to distinguish nominal strings/cash value from physical note count.
