## 3. Confirmed weaknesses (multiple personas converging)

- **Chapter 8 ("The Great Forgetting") is the manuscript's load-bearing weak chapter for non-specialist readers.** Editor: Attwood "reads as a function — the man who was right and was dismissed — more than as Backwell or Volcker do," and Ch. 8's analytical density "competes with Attwood's biographical line and partly buries it." Lay reader: drifted at ~p. 230, "the eighth subsection about Lord Overstone's letters and George Warde Norman's 51-year directorship." Heterodox reviewer: argumentatively this is the strongest chapter, but it carries the *argument*, not the *man*. Three independent reads naming the same chapter with different vocabulary.
- **Chapter 12 is overloaded.** Editor, historian, and lay reader all flag it. Currently carries: Volcker disinflation + crypto/Hayek-Selgin-Ammous + fiscal dominance + the knowledge problem + the institutional architecture proposal + Klemperer hyperinflation set piece + productive-capacity framework. Convergent prescription: 10–15% prune; let human scenes carry more.
- **Chapter-end "modern parallel" rhythm has become formulaic.** Editor's specific note. Greece 2010, Lehman 2008, COVID 2020, China today, Greece 2015 — once you notice it, you can't unnotice it.
- **No propulsive narrative spine.** Lay reader: "fourteen excellent essays stapled to a thesis statement." Heterodox: "no single forcing argument the bestsellers in the category each have." This is structural and probably not addressable without reframing the book — see strategic tension below.
- **Notes on Sources is uneven by chapter.** Historian: deeper for Chs. 1, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12; thinner for Chs. 2, 6.

## 4. The strategic tension the round surfaced

The heterodox reviewer and the editor are pulling in opposite directions, and the manuscript cannot satisfy both:

- **Heterodox path (Kelton/Graeber-tier breakout, 250k+ ceiling):** reinstate something like the cut MMT appendix, sharpen the productive-capacity claim so it is not immediately hedged by Sargent-Wallace and Cochrane, name a positive policy program (job guarantee, functional finance), critique central-bank independence on distributional grounds, name who benefits from the orthodox framing.
- **Establishment-credible path (Ahamed/Tooze-tier well-reviewed, 30–80k baseline → 80–150k with platform):** keep the steelmanning intact, keep the institutional balance, accept that "balance does not sell," and rely on the Tooze-class blurb / *Atlantic* excerpt / Treasury-Secretary-could-be-photographed-reading-it positioning.

The book is currently engineered for the second path. Reinstating the forcing argument would buy back the Kelton-tier blurber endorsement at the cost of the editor's "steelmanning is genuine" praise — these are not additive. **This is a strategic choice the author has to make consciously, not a craft decision.**

4. **Forcing-argument decision.** Strategic, not craft. See §4.

## 6. Prompt calibration — what to keep, what to fix

The v3 prompt produced useful triangulation but had three calibration issues worth fixing for the next round:

- **The lay reader prompt biased the rating downward by ~half a star.** Specifically: "she must be willing to rate it three stars or below if the book did not work for her" plus "at what page or chapter she most felt herself drifting" jointly nudge toward critical posture. The 3.5 is partly prompt-shaped; a more neutrally-prompted reader would likely have come in at 4.0 with the same substantive complaints. The *content* of her critique is real signal (corroborated by the editor); the *star rating* itself is a noisy instrument under that framing. Future prompt should ask for honest reaction without pre-loading willingness to be negative.
- **The verdict-binary structure (acquire / acquire-with-revisions / pass; original / competent / not-for-specialists) is too coarse to track movement between rounds.** A revision that genuinely improves five things and worsens one will hold its verdict, masking the underlying signal. Multi-dimensional scoring with reasoning per dimension would surface the texture better.
- **The diagnostic framing ("did threading land?") was right for v3 but is too narrow as a default.** Future rounds should ask broader multi-dimensional questions and let the reviewer surface the highest-leverage issues themselves rather than testing a pre-specified hypothesis.

What worked and should be kept:

- Spawning four personas in parallel with distinct remits.
- Persona-native deliverable formats (memo, essay-review, Goodreads, blurber-style).
- Requirement to read in full the chapters where the diagnostic question actually lives.
- The bestseller-tier craft question as a forcing function on each persona.

## 7. What the next round should test

Open questions v3 did not resolve, in priority order:

1. **Has the Ch. 8 craft pass landed for a non-specialist reader?** This is the single most important diagnostic for the next round. If yes, the lay reader rating moves to 4+ and the editor verdict-with-conditions becomes a clean acquire with no conditions.
2. **Does Ch. 12 read better at reduced length?** Test against editor and lay reader specifically.
3. **What is the right strategic call on the forcing argument?** This is not a question to delegate to another persona round — it is an author decision. But a second heterodox read after any sharpening would test whether the sharpening preserves establishment credibility.
4. **Does the prose hold up at the line level?** No round so far has asked specifically about sentence-level craft, voice consistency, or chapter-opening hooks. A craft-focused read would surface this.
5. **Does the book work as audio?** A non-trivial commercial channel (Audible) and one this manuscript has not been tested for.
