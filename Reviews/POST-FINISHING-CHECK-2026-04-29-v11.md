### Chapter 12 Capacity-Stress

**Adequately balanced.**

The capacity-stress section now gives 2021-22 the right shape: not a clean demand-only episode, not a clean supply-only episode, but a collision between elevated demand and damaged supply. The Stehlik and Nephew examples work because they show both sides at human/business scale: a nominal wage gain erased by household prices, and a small publisher forced to absorb or pass through logistics costs. That makes the inflation argument concrete without reducing it to slogan.

The new comparison with 2008 is especially important and now lands clearly. In 2008, the problem was broken balance sheets plus idle capacity; in 2021, many household balance sheets were stronger while the system that converted income into goods, housing, energy, transport, and services had been damaged. That distinction is exactly the conceptual correction the chapter needed.

The orthodox steelman section is also stronger. Buchanan-Wagner and Sargent-Wallace are treated as real warnings rather than straw men. The chapter concedes that Blanchard and Summers were directionally right on ARP scale, while preserving the broader point that the half-century record does not support a simple "democratic fiat always over-issues" story. That is the paired-vindication posture at its best.

Verdict: no substantive issue remains. A copyedit can still reduce data density and freeze current CPI/PCE/gasoline figures, but the argument is balanced enough.
