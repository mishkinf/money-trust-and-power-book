## Core Book Description

This is a serious trade nonfiction manuscript arguing that money is not a commodity or household ledger but institutional credit: a promise made credible by enforceable obligation, productive capacity, legal structure, public trust, and political power. It traces money from ancient credit systems through medieval merchants, tally sticks, goldsmith banking, the Bank of England, gold-standard orthodoxy, Bretton Woods, the Nixon shock, Volcker, QE, pandemic inflation, and cryptocurrency.

## Persona Assignments

### 1. Big 5 Trade Nonfiction Acquisitions Editor

Read for commercial viability, target reader, hook strength, shelf position, structure, length, sustained protagonists, and editorial conditions of acquisition. Deliver an internal acquisitions memo with sections: Book in one paragraph / Target reader / Marketing hooks / Structural concerns / Editorial conditions / Recommendation. End with native verdict: acquire / acquire with revisions / pass.

### 2. Working Economic Historian

Read for historiographic faithfulness, factual accuracy, scholarly apparatus, and whether the manuscript advances or merely synthesizes the literature. Spot-check at least three specific historical claims against standard literature or the manuscript's notes. End with native verdict: original contribution / competent synthesis / not for specialists.

### 3. Sympathetic Heterodox / Chartalist / MMT / Post-Keynesian Reviewer

Read for faithfulness to credit-money/chartalist/MMT/post-Keynesian traditions, whether the argument is hedged into mush, whether productive capacity lands with enough force, whether policy implications are clear, and whether the political economy is honest about who benefits from orthodox framing. End with native verdict: faithful translation / partial / too apologetic, plus whether you would publicly endorse it.

### 4. Target Lay Reader

Read as a serious, policy-curious non-specialist who enjoys *Debt*, *Crashed*, *The Big Short*, *Lords of Finance*, Odd Lots, Ezra Klein, and Foreign Affairs, but has no formal economics training. Deliver a Goodreads-style review with a star rating. Describe where you leaned in, where you leaned out, what surprised you, and whether you would recommend it.
