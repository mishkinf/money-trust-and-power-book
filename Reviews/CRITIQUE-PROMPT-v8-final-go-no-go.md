## Required Reviewer Stance

Answer in your assigned persona. Be candid, specific, and useful. Do not flatter. Do not assume previous issues have or have not been fixed. Do not reference previous critique rounds.

This review should not generate preference churn. You are deciding whether the manuscript is ready to leave substantive revision.

## Decision Rule

Only **P1 issues** should justify more substantive manuscript revision.

- **P1:** Blocks must-read / 5-of-5 status or creates real publication-confidence risk.
- **P2:** Meaningfully improves the book but should normally move to copyedit/proposal prep unless it reveals a trust problem.
- **P3:** Copy/cadence/local polish; do not recommend substantive revision for P3.

If you find no true P1s, say clearly: **No further substantive revision recommended.**

## No Generic Critique Rule

If you have critiques, do not give only generic categories like "tighten the middle" or "clarify the policy payoff."

For each critique, provide:

- **Location:** chapter and, where possible, section heading or quoted phrase.
- **What you were thinking as a reader/reviewer:** the actual hesitation, impatience, doubt, confusion, or missed opportunity.
- **Why it matters:** what it does to trust, momentum, clarity, commercial potential, or must-read status.
- **Concrete fix direction:** cut, compress, move, add, reframe, caveat, copyedit, source-check, or leave but sharpen.

If a section is excellent, say so specifically, but praise should not substitute for diagnosis.
