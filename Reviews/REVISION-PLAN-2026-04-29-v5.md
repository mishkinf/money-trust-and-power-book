The central revision is **structural compression and craft-trust in the final third**, organized around a single governing question. A parallel **specialist precision pass** addresses factual tripwires the second round surfaced. Everything else is downstream of those two moves.

- A. Cross-cutting moves (apply throughout)

**A5. Composite-figure replacement.** Replace named composite figures (Thomas Fletcher in Ch. 5; "Farmer A" in Ch. 9) with documented archival individuals at the Backwell/Todd standard. If archival material isn't recoverable, mark the figure as composite explicitly and compress.

## C. Final-third rebuild

The single largest piece of revision work. All eight reviewers flagged engagement-curve sag in 12A / 12B / Epilogue. The fix is structural, not cosmetic: one governing question, four escalating cases, one resolution.

### Governing question

Open the consolidated Ch. 12 with the question the orthodox tradition keeps asking the book:

> *If gold was never the foundation, what keeps fiat money from becoming fantasy?*

Then 2008, 2021, Volcker, and crypto become escalating tests of that question. Each test attaches one orthodox steelman as the worry it interrogates. The Epilogue provides the answer the four cases have accumulated.

### Why this structure

It dissolves D3 (12A/12B architecture) — the question has one ascent, not validation-then-tests. It clarifies D2 (Klemperer destination) — Weimar isn't a discrete case in the four-case structure; it lives upstream. It pushes D1 (Position 7) — a closing scene cannot carry seven positions, so Position 7 either relocates to Ch. 10 or cuts to a footnote. The four-case structure also tightens the steelman discipline: instead of a discrete Buchanan/Sargent-Wallace/Cochrane/Hayek section, each critique attaches to the case that tests it.

### Ch. 12 — four-case ascent

Open with the governing question. Then:

**Case 1 — 2008 (with Japan folded in): money creation without consumer inflation.**
- Steelman tested: Friedman-Schwartz's monetary-contraction worry (already vindicated by Bernanke; the case shows what *capacity slack* added to the picture).
- Human anchors: McKenzie, Spero, Kwalwasser carry the acute Western version. Kono carries the chronic Japanese version.
- Argument: 2008 is the acute Western form of the experiment Japan has been running chronically since the 1990s. Same finding: massive central-bank balance-sheet expansion does not produce CPI inflation when transmission runs through asset markets and capacity is slack.
- Operational walk-through (Treasury General Account, reserve mechanics) belongs here, attached to the 2008 evidence rather than free-floating.

**Case 2 — 2021: money creation meeting real capacity limits.**
- Steelmans tested: Buchanan-Wagner public-choice (political bias toward loose money) + Sargent-Wallace fiscal-dominance worry.
- Human anchor: one or two specific 2021 households or businesses bearing the inflation. (Manuscript currently has port-congestion and chip-shortage decompositions; these need to compress around a person.)
- Argument: the heterodox "transitory" posture aged poorly; Blanchard and Summers were directionally right; the episode was a layered demand-plus-supply event where capacity bottlenecks were real. This is the most honest version of the case the book can make and the one round-2 heterodox reviewer specifically endorsed.

**Case 3 — Volcker: the human cost of restoring credibility.**
- Steelman tested: the Currency School's discretionary-issuance worry, tested through the Argentina/Brazil counterfactual.
- Human anchors: the Hagedorns at Spencer; the passbook-saver retiree on the other side of the ledger.
- Argument: paired vindication at its hardest. Volcker prevented one cruelty and imposed another. The political system was structurally biased toward deferring the concentrated cruelty. Argentina and Brazil show what the alternative looked like when fiscal-dominance went unchecked.
- **Critical:** rewrite Sargent-Wallace / Cochrane in the book's own vocabulary here. Tax capacity, real-resource governance, institutional legitimacy, inflation anchoring — not "long-run primary surpluses sufficient to back the debt path." Round-2 heterodox: most important single fix in the section.

**Case 4 — crypto: the fantasy of escaping institutions.**
- Steelman tested: Hayek's knowledge problem (which is exactly what crypto claims to solve, and exactly what it has not solved).
- Human anchor: one specific crypto-collapse story (FTX customer, Terra/Luna holder, mining-town casualty) — needs new material if not in current draft.
- Argument: the institutional critique embedded in crypto is real; the architectural answer fails on the same grounds the gold standard failed. The book's framework predicts this without dismissing the worry.

### Epilogue — resolution

- One paragraph stating the answer the four cases have produced: what keeps fiat from becoming fantasy is institutional capacity, real resources, political legitimacy, and willingness to honor distributional choices.
- 3–4 implications maximum, not 7. Fold Position 4 (Fed dual mandate) into Position 6 (institutional answer to public choice) — same argument, round-1 historian. Resolve Position 7 per D1 (recommended: cut to footnote or relocate to Ch. 10).
- Add Tcherneva-style job-guarantee design parameters to the institutional-answer position — round-1 heterodox: federal funding, fixed minimum wage, available to anyone willing to work, integration with existing safety net (R8).

It is who has the power to write the ledger, who must honor it, and who pays when institutions pretend scarcity is natural.

Candidates: Backwell's 1684 exhumation (the man whose private ledger the public ledger absorbed); Todd's child grown in postwar Britain after the gold standard fell; Kono finally leaving his parents' house; one of the 2021 anchors looking at the inflation cooling. Pick the scene that closes the loop the manuscript opened.

## D. Research prompts

For each item: **always grep the existing research folder first.** The memory note is correct that things have been sourced and missed before. Where the research folder is silent, dispatch the prompt.

### R1 — Bank notes and tax receivability during the Restriction
> Verify the manuscript's claim that during the 1797–1821 Bank Restriction, tax payments to the Crown were made in Bank of England notes. Specifically: (a) what was the legal status of Bank of England notes between 1797 and 1821 — were they legal tender, were they specifically receivable for tax, and through what mechanism? (b) When did Bank of England notes become legal tender (the conventional answer is 1833); what intermediate statutes changed receivability? (c) What was the actual operational tax-payment mechanism in 1810: coin, country bank notes, Bank notes, bills of exchange, settlement at the Exchequer? Cite Feavearyear *The Pound Sterling*, Clapham *The Bank of England: A History*, Fetter *Development of British Monetary Orthodoxy*, and the relevant statute (39 Geo. III c. 1; 56 Geo. III c. 68; 3 & 4 Will. IV c. 98). The chartalist claim must survive — the goal is operational precision, not retreat from the thesis.

### R2 — Newton's 1717 mint ratio: gold over- or undervalued?
> Verify Ch. 6's language on Newton's 1717 mint adjustment. England's mint ratio after Newton's adjustment overvalued gold relative to silver at the new official price (21 shillings for the guinea, equivalent to ~£3 17s 10½d per troy ounce of gold), which drew gold in and drove full-weight silver coin out as melt or export. Confirm the manuscript's wording uses this direction, not the inverse. Cite Feavearyear, Li *The Great Recoinage of 1696*, Eichengreen *Globalizing Capital* Ch. 2, and Kindleberger *A Financial History of Western Europe* on the bimetallic ratio history. Standard secondary literature is unambiguous on direction.

### R3 — Bank of England note circulation 1814 vs 1821
> Verify the manuscript's figure that Bank of England note circulation fell from £28.4M in 1814 to £2.3M in 1821. The £2.3M figure looks suspect for total Bank of England circulation — that may be the figure for £1 and £2 notes (the small-denomination class authorized during the Restriction and withdrawn after resumption), not total notes in circulation. Total Bank of England circulation in the post-Restriction years was on the order of £20–25M. Pull the actual figures from Clapham *Bank of England*, Vol. II, the Cope tables, or the Bank's own *Statistical Abstract*; correct the body text and Notes accordingly.

### R4 — Sterling parity wording in 1925
> Confirm Ch. 9's wording on the 1925 return to gold. The conventional figure is the pre-war sterling/dollar parity of $4.86 per pound sterling — that is the dollar price of sterling, not the dollar price of gold. The dollar price of gold was $20.67 per troy ounce. Sterling's gold parity was 113 grains of fine gold or £4 4s 11½d per troy ounce. Verify the manuscript's wording does not collapse "$4.86 per ounce of gold" — that compound is incorrect. Cite Moggridge *British Monetary Policy 1924–1931*, Skidelsky *Keynes Vol. II*, Ahamed *Lords of Finance*.

### R5 — Todd lockout chronology
> Verify Ch. 9's chronology of the Todd family experience at Boldon Colliery. Standard chronology: General Strike May 4–12 1926; coal lockout May 1 – November 30 1926 (seven months). The 1925–26 broader coal crisis includes the Red Friday subsidy of July 1925 and the Samuel Commission of March 1926. Confirm the manuscript distinguishes the seven-month 1926 lockout from the longer 1925–26 industrial-relations crisis, and that dated details (the February 1926 baby; hessian-bag shoes; sparrow snares) sit within the lockout period. Cite Phillips *General Strike*, Renshaw *Nine Days in May*, Briggs/Miller history of the Durham coalfield if available.

### R6 — Documented Bank of England loan recipient circa 1700–1720
> The manuscript currently uses a composite figure named "Thomas Fletcher" in Ch. 5 to illustrate Bank of England lending mechanics in the early eighteenth century. Identify a documented loan recipient from the Bank's early ledgers (1694–1720) whose loan, repayment, and use of funds can be reconstructed at the Backwell standard — i.e., date, amount, security, balance-sheet effect, and one human detail. Bank of England Archive: ledgers ADM 1–10, Discount Office records, and the Court Books are the primary sources. Quinn's *Goldsmith-Bankers and the Bank of England* and the Bank's tercentenary publications (Ackrill/Hannah, Roberts/Kynaston) may surface a usable individual. If no individual at the right level of granularity exists, return that finding so we can mark the figure as composite or replace with an institutional micro-narrative.

### R7 — Helleiner's institutional-design argument on Bretton Woods
> Summarize Eric Helleiner's institutional-design reading of Bretton Woods (his *States and the Reemergence of Global Finance* and *Forgotten Foundations of Bretton Woods*) at a granularity sufficient to integrate one to two paragraphs into Ch. 10's body — not just the Notes. The point is to register the choice between Steil's procedural-control reading and Helleiner's institutional-design reading symmetrically in the chapter, rather than letting the body be Steil-aligned and the Notes flag the disagreement. Specifically: what does Helleiner argue White and the U.S. Treasury were trying to build for the post-war development order, and how does that change the reading of the conference's outcomes?

### R8 — Tcherneva job-guarantee design parameters
> Pull from Pavlina Tcherneva's *The Case for a Job Guarantee* and her Levy Institute working papers the design parameters the modern MMT tradition specifies for a federal job guarantee: (a) funding source (federal); (b) wage floor and benefits package; (c) eligibility (universal, voluntary); (d) administration (decentralized via state/local/nonprofit project sponsors); (e) integration with existing safety net (UI, SNAP, Medicaid); (f) macroeconomic stabilizer mechanism (counter-cyclical employment buffer). One paragraph that lands these points cleanly will let the Epilogue's institutional-answer position stop deferring to "Tcherneva has done this work" and actually cite the design.

### R9 — Crypto-collapse human anchor for Case 4
> Identify one documented human casualty of a major cryptocurrency collapse (FTX November 2022, Terra/Luna May 2022, Celsius/BlockFi/Voyager 2022, or a 2021–22 mining-town bust) at the Backwell/Todd/Hagedorn/McKenzie standard — i.e., named individual, dated events, specific financial loss, one human detail that lets the abstract critique of "the fantasy of escaping institutions" land in a body. Court filings (FTX bankruptcy customer claims; Terra-Luna class actions), reporting in *The New York Times*, *Wall Street Journal*, *Bloomberg*, *Wired*, and Zeke Faux's *Number Go Up* are the most likely sources. The figure must work for the four-case structure of Ch. 12 — Hayek's knowledge problem tested against a real institutional collapse.

### R10 — Klemperer/Aunt-Berta salt-herring sourcing detail (low priority)
> The Klemperer hyperinflation set-piece is currently strong but if it moves to the Preface or Ch. 9 opening, double-check sourcing for Eva Klemperer's coffee-counter scene, the Berta salt-herring memory, and the Preuß family arc against Klemperer's diaries (*Ich will Zeugnis ablegen* / *I Shall Bear Witness*) and any Preuß family secondary sources. The relocation invites more reader scrutiny than the current placement.

### R11 — 2021 inflation human anchor for Case 2
> Identify one or two documented households or small businesses whose 2021–22 experience can carry the "money creation meeting real capacity limits" case at the Backwell/Todd/Hagedorn standard. Candidates: a household whose budget the inflation specifically broke; a small-business operator on the supply side of a port-congestion or chip-shortage bottleneck; a worker whose nominal-vs-real wage trajectory illustrates the squeeze. Reporting from *NYT*, *WaPo*, ProPublica, *The Atlantic*, *NPR* on 2021–22 household inflation experience is the source pool. The figure must let Buchanan-Wagner public-choice and Sargent-Wallace fiscal-dominance worries land in a body, not just a chart.

**Pass 4 — Compression and structure pass (5–8 days, the main work).**
1. Preface revisions — paired-vindication contract; Klemperer relocation if D2 = Preface.
2. Part I scene-craft + cut (Chs. 1, 2).

5. **Final-third rebuild** (§C above). Largest single piece of work. Sub-steps:
   - Open Ch. 12 with the governing question.
   - Build Case 1 (2008 + Japan): re-home operational walk-through; integrate McKenzie/Spero/Kwalwasser/Kono; attach Friedman-Schwartz steelman.
   - Build Case 2 (2021): compress port-congestion/chip-shortage data; integrate human anchor (R11); attach Buchanan-Wagner + Sargent-Wallace steelmans, rewritten in chartalist vocabulary.
   - Build Case 3 (Volcker): lift counterfactual forward; attach Currency School + fiscal-dominance steelmans rewritten in chartalist vocabulary; Hagedorn anchor stays.
   - Build Case 4 (crypto): integrate human anchor (R9); attach Hayek knowledge-problem steelman.
