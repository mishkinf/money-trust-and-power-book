# Structural Threading Plan: From Strong-Recommend to Must-Read

**Status:** proposed but not yet executed. Recommended sequencing: run the v2 persona critique first (`Reviews/CRITIQUE-PROMPT-v2.md`), then revisit this plan with the verdict comparison in hand to confirm targets and decide which figures to thread.

---

## The diagnosis

The five-persona critique at the start of this session converged on one structural finding: the book has many figures across many chapters but no sustained protagonist whose interior life carries the reader the way Liaquat Ahamed's Norman/Strong/Schacht/Moreau carry *Lords of Finance* or Michael Lewis's Burry/Eisman/Lippmann carry *The Big Short*. The editor persona named this most directly:

> "There is no single sustained character whose interior life carries the reader the way Ahamed's Norman or Lewis's Burry does. What holds it back is craft-level rather than conceptual: voice consistency, length, and a developmental editor's hand on the thesis-restating tic. With a 90k-word expansion that deepens three or four central figures into Ahamed-grade portraits, and with a Kelton-tier platform behind it, this clears the bar."

The session's revisions (revisions 59–62) addressed every other persona-flagged issue — the analytical fairness, the Currency School steelman, the 2021 narrative reconstruction, the historiographic care, the citation apparatus. The structural ceiling is the only one that remains untouched.

## Why this wasn't already factored in

Phase 3 in the proposed-revisions doc (`Reviews/PROPOSED-REVISIONS-2026-04-28.md`) was scoped as "extend existing scenes within the current chapter structure" — additive set-piece deepening, not architectural change. With must-read as an explicit target, the deeper structural move comes into scope. It was deferred earlier because re-architecting a manuscript through 62 revisions felt like overstepping; with the target now stated, the calculation flips.

## What the move actually is

**Not full restructuring (Ahamed model).** Ahamed picked four contemporaries facing the same 1919–1939 crisis. *Money, Trust, and Power* covers 5,000 years; no four contemporaries span it. A full Ahamed-style restructure isn't available because the source material doesn't have that shape.

**Threaded protagonists (achievable).** Pick 3–5 figures who naturally span 2–4 chapters and thread their interior lives across their full arcs rather than compressing them to single set pieces. The existing material already supports four candidate threads, with a fifth available:

### Candidate threads

**Edward Backwell (1618–1683)** — currently anchors Ch.4 (the Stop, the Sunday Treasury meeting, the post-1672 deepening into the Netherlands exile). Available threading:
- Ch.3 (sovereign default risk): Backwell's pre-1672 lending to the Crown, the Pepys diary entries, the Lombard Street culture of risk that the chapter currently treats abstractly.
- Ch.4 (current): the catastrophe.
- Ch.5 (Bank of England solution): explicitly framed as the institutional answer to what destroyed Backwell. The Bank's 1694 charter design — parliamentary backing, lender-of-last-resort role — addresses the failure modes Backwell experienced. Position the Bank's founding as the answer to a question Backwell's ruin had posed twenty-two years earlier.

**William Paterson (1658–1719)** — currently anchors Ch.5 (Cameronian background, Caribbean years, Bank founding, Darien tragedy). Available threading:
- Ch.4 brief mention: the projector ecology around the Stop, Paterson's earlier failed 1691 bank proposal.
- Ch.5 (current): full founding scene.
- Ch.6 (Newton 1717): Paterson's death in 1719 just before Newton's adjustment; he never saw the gold standard his Bank's success inadvertently produced.
- Ch.10 (Bretton Woods): Keynes's bancor / International Clearing Union as the realization of Paterson's projector-tradition vision of supranational credit. The chapter could explicitly note the lineage: Paterson dreamed at continental scale; Keynes proposed at global scale; both were defeated by the same kind of national-power calculus.

**Thomas Attwood (1783–1856)** — currently anchors Ch.8 (Birmingham Political Union, Newhall Hill rally, Chartist petition). Available threading:
- Ch.7 (Bullionist controversy): Attwood as a young Birmingham manufacturer watching the 1821 deflation destroy his workers; the chapter currently mentions him only briefly (the "Attila the Hun" quote). Could open Ch.7 with Attwood at twenty-eight in his manufactory.
- Ch.8 (current): his political defeat.
- Ch.9 (Churchill 1925): the Todd family at Boldon Colliery framed as Attwood-vindicated-too-late; the 1926 General Strike as the disaster Attwood predicted in 1832 arriving on schedule.

**Paul Volcker (1927–2019)** — currently anchors a section of Ch.12 (the 1979–82 disinflation, the "two patterns of cruelty" framing, the Hagedorn farm). Available threading:
- Ch.10 (Bretton Woods): young Volcker as a graduate student at Harvard during the conference, then early Treasury career. He spent his life inside the system the chapter is describing.
- Ch.11 (Breaking of Bretton Woods): Volcker as Under Secretary of the Treasury during the August 1971 weekend; he was actually in the room at Camp David. Quote his memoir on what he saw: "If the British were going to take gold for their dollars, it was clear the game was indeed over."
- Ch.12 (current): 1979–82 disinflation.

The Volcker thread connects the post-WWII institutional architecture to its near-collapse to its discipline-imposed continuation. It's the spine the post-1944 chapters currently lack.

**John Maynard Keynes (1883–1946)** — currently anchors Ch.10. Available threading:
- Ch.7 (Bullionist controversy): Keynes's intellectual descent from Henry Thornton, the Real Bills tradition Keynes would later overturn.
- Ch.9 (Churchill 1925): *Economic Consequences of Mr. Churchill* as the chapter's central document — pull more of Keynes's text into the narrative.
- Ch.10 (current): Bretton Woods, the bancor, the heart attack on the hotel stairs.

The Keynes thread is the lightest add — he's already heavily voiced in Ch.10. The Ch.7 and Ch.9 mentions would be brief.

## Realistic scope

- **3–4 figures threaded** is probably the right number. Five risks fragmentation; three may not be enough.
- **+3,000 to +5,000 words** added across multiple chapters.
- **Some compression** in existing material to maintain target length (~72k–75k body).
- **2–3 sessions** of focused threading work. Not a multi-month editorial project.
- **One light second review pass** to verify the threading lands (probably just editor + historian personas, focused reads).

## Recommended sequencing

1. **First**: run the v2 persona critique (`Reviews/CRITIQUE-PROMPT-v2.md`). Verdict comparison against the original critique. Surfaces what moved and what didn't, plus any non-structural issues we haven't anticipated.
2. **Then**: based on which figures the new personas independently name as memorable, decide on 3–4 threading targets. (My current best guess: Backwell, Paterson, Attwood, Volcker. The reviews may shift this.)
3. **Then**: execute the threading work, chapter by chapter, with research-grounded character material from the existing research base (the `archival character research.md` file lists what's available for each anchor).
4. **Then**: light register-consistency pass on the threaded sections.
5. **Then**: optional light second review (editor + historian) to verify threading.
6. **Ship.**

## Why this might lift the book

- **NYT reviewer**: a sustained protagonist (or protagonists) is the specific lever the persona named. Threading 3–4 figures across multiple chapters supplies the "character oxygen" the persona faulted.
- **Editor**: the editor's "no Norman or Burry" diagnosis is what threading addresses directly. With the threading done, the editor's verdict has the chance to move from "yes-with-revisions" to a clean acquire.
- **Historian**: the threading deepens the historical figures the historian would already verify against archival sources — strengthens the manuscript without weakening any factual claim.
- **Conservative critic / Economist**: threading mostly orthogonal to their concerns; the threading could go either way for them.

The bet is that the threading carries the manuscript from "strong recommend on craft" to "must read" by adding the structural feature five-thousand-year-history-with-set-pieces lacks compared to Ahamed-style focused-period-with-protagonists.

## Risk

The threading could fail if:
- The figures don't actually carry across multiple chapters in their available historical record (we'd find this out chapter by chapter).
- The threading material competes with rather than supports the institutional argument (manageable through careful drafting).
- The added length pushes the book past its target (manageable through compression elsewhere).

None of these are showstoppers; they're things to watch.

## What to do if the v2 reviews come back differently than predicted

- **If the reviews come back at must-read level already**: don't do the threading. Ship.
- **If the reviews come back below strong-recommend**: address the new issues first; threading is a craft enhancement, not a fix for analytical or historical problems.
- **If the reviews come back at strong-recommend with the structural ceiling explicitly named (most likely outcome)**: execute the threading.
