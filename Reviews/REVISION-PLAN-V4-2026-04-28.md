## Goal

Move the manuscript from the **well-reviewed** tier to the **must-read** tier in one revision pass focused on three convergent items: Preface, Chapter 8, and Chapter 12. Expected v4-instrument shifts after this pass:

- Editor: ACQUIRE WITH REVISIONS → clean ACQUIRE
- Lay reader: 4 stars → 4.5
- Heterodox: FAITHFUL TRANSLATION + endorsement-with-caveat → endorsement without caveat
- Historian: ORIGINAL CONTRIBUTION held, with policy-clarity dimension lifting from 3.5 toward 4.5

This is a **craft pass**, not a research pass. No new archives. No new historical reconstruction. The existing material is reorganized, compressed, and in one place (Ch. 12 institutional architecture) extended to match the strength of the concessions the book has already made.

A separate follow-on pass — prose-level voice consolidation and character interiority on post-1944 figures — would close the remaining distance to breakout tier (Kelton/Graeber-tier 250k+ ceiling). That pass is **out of scope** for this revision.

## What this plan does *not* do

- It does not reinstate an MMT appendix or any forcing-argument appendix. The v4 round established that the Preface and the institutional-architecture buildout in Ch. 12B can do the policy-clarity work an appendix would have done, additively, without costing the steelmanning balance.
- It does not change the four-part architecture (Origins / Institutional Revolution / Gold Standard / Breaking and Beyond). The architecture survives every reviewer's read.
- It does not vary the chapter-coda "modern parallel" rhythm at every chapter end. The editor's note is real but it is a tier-3 fix; this pass concentrates leverage on the tier-1 convergent items.
- It does not change the opening of Chapter 1. The lay reader's "open with Backwell instead of Uruk" prescription conflicts with the historian's structural read; treat as commercial/audience-fit input rather than structural mandate. Revisit only if a follow-on pass surfaces it again.

### Pass 3 — Chapter 12 split + institutional-architecture buildout (two to three sessions)

**Goal:** split the overloaded chapter; expand the institutional-architecture section to match the strength of the concessions the book has already made; preserve the Volcker thread across the new boundary.

**Specific changes:**

1. **Split into 12A and 12B.**
    - **12A "The Validation: 2008–2020"** carries the productive-capacity case as validated by the 2008 Fed response, the COVID 2020 mobilization, and the Hayek-Selgin-Ammous engagement (cryptocurrency does not solve the institutional-credibility problem; it relocates it). Ends on the cryptocurrency engagement at full force.
    - **12B "The Tests"** carries the harder cases: the 2021 inflation honest reckoning (no framework called it cleanly, including the productive-capacity framework); the Volcker counterfactual against Argentina (two patterns of cruelty, the chronic and diffuse one would have fallen on retirees and wage-earners without indexed pensions); the public-choice / fiscal-dominance / knowledge-problem trilogy; and the institutional-architecture buildout (see #2). Ends on the Epilogue handoff.
   The split point is roughly where the current chapter pivots from "the framework was validated" to "but the framework owes its critics an answer." Identify exact split during baseline read.

2. **Build the institutional-architecture section in 12B into a full 1,500–2,000-word treatment.** Currently a paragraph. The convergent reviewer prescription (historian + heterodox specifically) names what to include:
    - **Symmetric fiscal rules.** Built-in surplus requirements during productive-capacity-saturated periods, not just deficit ceilings during slack. Why symmetry matters: the public-choice critique is about discretionary politics, and asymmetric rules permit discretion in only one direction.
    - **Independent fiscal council with binding non-inflationary-envelope authority.** Not advisory. Not a second OBR. The council estimates non-inflationary fiscal capacity in real time and the legislature operates inside that envelope, with override permitted only by named supermajority.
    - **Pre-legislated automatic stabilizers triggered at named inflation thresholds.** Tax-and-transfer triggers that activate without legislative action when inflation crosses defined bands, removing the political discretion that public-choice critiques target.
    - **Sunset clauses on discretionary spending.** Programs created in slack periods sunset automatically unless re-authorized in the next non-slack period.
    - **Evaluated against three contemporary cases:**
      - The EU's revised fiscal framework (post-2024 reform; the asymmetric weakness)
      - The UK's OBR (the right institutional posture, the wrong authority — advisory rather than binding)
      - The Chilean structural balance rule (post-2001; the closest extant approximation, where it works and where it fails)

    Acceptance criterion: a Buchanan-Wagner reader who finishes 12B knows what specific institutional architecture would close the public-choice critique, and what real-world cases approximate it.

3. **Preserve the Volcker thread cleanly across the 12A/12B boundary.** Volcker has been the book's structural protagonist across Chs. 10/11. The Hagedorn farm, the Argentina counterfactual, and the disinflation ledger now sit in 12B. Verify in the final coherence pass that the Volcker arc still reads as continuous from his 1949 Princeton thesis through Camp David through the 1979–82 disinflation, with the 12A/12B boundary running between the validation chapter and the test chapter rather than splitting Volcker himself.

4. **Land the Epilogue handoff.** The Epilogue currently follows Ch. 12. After the split, the Epilogue follows 12B. Verify the handoff still works — and if the heterodox reviewer's tier-3 suggestion (rewrite the Epilogue from a six-lessons recap into a single argumentative essay confronting the three open questions) reads as compelling after this pass, add it as a follow-on. Default: leave the Epilogue alone in this pass.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- 12A and 12B each function as standalone chapters; neither feels like half a chapter.
- The institutional-architecture section reads as a worked treatment, not a list.
- A Buchanan-Wagner reader, a Cochrane reader, and a Hayek-Selgin-Ammous reader each finish 12B feeling their concerns were taken seriously and answered with specific institutional design rather than gesture.
- The Volcker thread is intact across the boundary.
- Total Ch. 12 length (12A + 12B combined) does not increase by more than ~10% net of the architecture buildout, because the trilogy (public-choice / fiscal-dominance / knowledge-problem) is compressed where it currently sprawls.

**Out of scope for Pass 3:** a new chapter on cryptocurrency (the lay reader gestured at it; the existing Hayek-Selgin-Ammous section in 12A absorbs the same material at the right register); rewriting the 2021 inflation reckoning (it is honest as it stands; the round praised it).

- **Convergent fix list shrinks.** v4's tier-1 items (Ch. 12, Ch. 8, Preface) drop off. The remaining items become the follow-on pass scope.
- **Editor verdict moves to ACQUIRE without conditions.**
- **Lay reader rating moves to 4.5.**
- **Heterodox endorsement loses the policy-architecture-underspecified caveat.**
- **Median policy clarity score moves from 3.5 to ~4.5.**
- **Median structural coherence stabilizes at 4.5+ across all four reviewers.**

If any of these don't move as predicted, that is signal that the pass missed its target on that dimension and a localized re-pass is warranted. If all of them move as predicted, the manuscript is in must-read territory and the follow-on craft pass is the path to breakout.
