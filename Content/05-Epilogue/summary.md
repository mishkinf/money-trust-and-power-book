# Epilogue Summary: From Gold to Governance

The Epilogue closes the book by arguing that monetary history is ultimately a history of governance. It distills five lessons from the chapters: money began in institutional accounting more than barter, taxation creates baseline demand, domestic and foreign money pose different problems, gold worked only through credible institutions, and fiat flexibility requires institutional discipline. The ending preserves the "paired vindication" frame, asks what the reader should now see differently about deficits, central-bank rescues, real resources, and institutional risk, then returns to Edward Backwell's reburial at Tyringham as the book's final image of private credit failing without enforceable public obligation.
