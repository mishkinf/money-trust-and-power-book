# Chapter 10 Summary: The Interwar Catastrophe

Churchill's 1925 return to gold at a 10% overvalued parity imposed devastating deflation on British workers, triggering the General Strike and seventeen months of miner suffering documented through families like Harry Todd's. France and the US hoarded gold, creating the Great Depression that destroyed lives from Durham coal miners to Iowa farmers facing foreclosure. Roosevelt's 1933 break from gold—nationalizing gold, devaluing the dollar, abrogating gold clauses—proved fiat money works when institutions are credible, with GDP growing 8% annually afterward, while countries that stayed on gold longer (like France until 1936) suffered needlessly extended agony.
