# Chapter 12 Summary: The Tests

The age of pure fiat did not remove monetary constraint; it changed where constraint lives. The chapter tests fiat money across four modern cases. The 2008 crisis and Japan's chronic quantitative easing show that money creation against abundant slack need not produce consumer inflation, especially when transmission runs through financial balance sheets rather than household income. The 2021-2022 inflation shows the opposite condition: public demand meeting damaged supply chains, energy shocks, housing constraints, and tight labor markets. Volcker's disinflation shows that credibility can be rebuilt through monetary discipline, but only by assigning acute costs to workers, farmers, and industrial regions. Cryptocurrency supplies the coda: the attempt to escape institutional trust through algorithmic scarcity ended up recreating exchanges, custody risk, leverage, bank runs, and legal recovery. The chapter's answer is that fiat money is disciplined by capacity, credibility, governance, and distributional legitimacy, not by metal.
