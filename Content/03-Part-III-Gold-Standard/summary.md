# Part III Summary: The Gold Standard Era and Its Contradictions

The gold standard emerged accidentally from Newton's 1717 miscalculation and became global orthodoxy by 1880, appearing self-regulating but actually requiring constant Bank of England management while imposing deflationary bias serving creditor interests. The Bank Restriction Act (1797-1821) proved paper money could work without gold backing, yet the 1821 return to gold caused devastating deflation that enriched creditors while destroying farmers—evidence systematically ignored. Between 1800-1850, earlier understandings of money creation were erased and replaced by Currency School orthodoxy that served bondholders' material interests, with dissenters like Thomas Attwood marginalized as "currency cranks" despite gathering 1.3 million signatures for monetary reform.
