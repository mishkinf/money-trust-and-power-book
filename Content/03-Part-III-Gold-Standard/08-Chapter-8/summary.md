# Chapter 8 Summary: The Great Forgetting

Between 1800-1850, earlier understandings that money was a social institution were systematically erased and replaced by gold orthodoxy that aligned with bondholders' material interests in deflation. Thomas Attwood and the Birmingham School correctly identified monetary contraction as causing distress and gathered 1.3 million Chartist petition signatures, but were dismissed as "currency cranks" as what had been explicit in 1694—banks creating money "out of nothing"—became unthinkable in respectable economics. This wasn't the discovery of truth but the consolidation of theories that systematically protected creditor wealth, with household budget metaphors masking the reality that governments issuing currency face fundamentally different constraints than households.
