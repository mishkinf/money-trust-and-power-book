# Chapter 6 Summary: Newton's Accident

Isaac Newton's 1717 adjustment to fix England's bimetallic currency accidentally created the gold standard by slightly undervaluing gold at the Mint, causing silver to drain from circulation over subsequent decades. The system that globalized by 1880 appeared automatic but required active Bank of England management through interest rate manipulation, as crises in 1825, 1890, and 1914 demonstrated. The gold standard's apparent success stemmed from British institutional credibility—Parliament's taxing power and the Bank's management skill—not from gold itself, revealing that monetary constraints are institutional choices rather than natural laws embedded in precious metal.
