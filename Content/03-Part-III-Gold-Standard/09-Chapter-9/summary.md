# Chapter 9 Summary: Manufacturing Orthodoxy

The Currency School captured Parliament through the 1844 Bank Charter Act despite losing the intellectual debate in publications like *The Economist*, demonstrating that orthodoxy is manufactured through institutional power rather than persuasion. Peel's Act attempted to end fractional reserve banking by restricting note issuance, but failed because economists didn't understand that banks could create money through deposits instead. The Act was suspended during crises in 1847, 1857, and 1866, yet the orthodoxy persisted—with devastating human costs documented through stories like Harold Heslop's unemployment after Churchill's 1925 return to gold—proving that creditor-serving monetary constraints can survive repeated empirical failure when embedded in law and institutions.
