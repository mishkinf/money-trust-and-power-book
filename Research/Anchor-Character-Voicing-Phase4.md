# Research request: anchor character voicing for Phase 4 prose pass

## Purpose

The NYT reviewer's main remaining complaint is character work — that several anchor figures appear in the manuscript as "names attached to balance sheets" rather than as voiced human beings. The chapters do significant biographical work already; what's missing is *direct quotation, contemporaneous physical detail, and one or two sentences of voice* per anchor on first introduction. Ahamed's *Lords of Finance* is the model: Norman, Strong, Schacht, Moreau emerge as full human beings before the policy argument lands.

The five anchors are already cited in the manuscript apparatus. Each section below names the existing citation and asks for specific extracts from those primary sources. The format follows the Hayek/Ammous/Selgin research doc: bullet-pointed quotes with page numbers, brief context, primary-source citations I can drop straight into `sources.md`.

**Total scope:** roughly 500–1,000 words of research notes. Doesn't need to be polished. Direct quotes (~30–60 words each) with page numbers are ideal.

**Priority order:** Volcker > Keynes > Attwood > Paterson > Backwell. Volcker is most accessible (Volcker's memoir is in print, easy to source). Backwell is hardest (the documentary sources are mostly ledgers, not letters).

---

## 1. Volcker (Ch. 12) — *highest priority, most accessible*

**Existing citation:** Volcker, Paul A., with Christine Harper. *Keeping At It: The Quest for Sound Money and Good Government.* New York: PublicAffairs, 2018. (Ch. 12 source 36a)

**What I need:**
- 2–3 direct quotes from the chapters covering 1979–82 — the period of the Saturday Night Special (October 6, 1979) and the sustained tightening that followed.
- Specifically useful: anything Volcker says about his own thinking, his fears, what he saw as the alternative. "What I was afraid of was..." or "I knew this would mean..." or "We could not afford to..."
- One quote on his interpretation of the Argentina/Brazil/inflation-expectations risk that the new counterfactual section in Ch. 12 makes central.
- One physical or biographical detail — what he looked like, what he sounded like, what his FOMC colleagues observed about him — would help. The "tall, cigar-smoking" Volcker is iconic; a contemporary observation would carry it.

**Where it would be deployed:** Ch. 12, Volcker section (currently lines ~247–267). The existing prose introduces him competently but he doesn't speak. One direct quote at his appointment + one in the middle of the disinflation + one looking back would transform the section.

## 2. Keynes at Bretton Woods (Ch. 10)

**Existing citations:**
- Skidelsky, Robert. *John Maynard Keynes: Fighting for Freedom, 1937–1946.* New York: Viking, 2000. (Ch. 11 sources 43c, 56)
- Robbins, Lionel. *The Wartime Diaries of Lionel Robbins and James Meade, 1943–45.* (Ch. 11 source 43a)
- Steil, Benn. *The Battle of Bretton Woods.* Princeton, 2013. (Ch. 11 source 43, 43b)

**What I need:**
- 2–3 direct quotes from Keynes himself (Skidelsky vol. 3 quotes letters and conversations extensively) about what Bretton Woods was for, what he feared losing, what he thought of White and the American team.
- One contemporary observation of Keynes's physical state at the conference — the heart attack on July 19, his exhaustion, his appearance during the negotiating sessions. Skidelsky has this in detail; Robbins's diaries have it too.
- One quote from Robbins's diaries on what it was like to negotiate alongside Keynes during the Mount Washington Hotel sessions.

**Where it would be deployed:** Ch. 10's Keynes-vs-White material. The chapter currently treats Keynes as a position rather than a person. The set piece of his collapse on the hotel stairs is the book's most cinematic scene per the NYT reviewer; it deserves a Keynes who is voiced beforehand.

## 3. Attwood (Ch. 8)

**Existing citations:**
- Moss, D. J. *Thomas Attwood: The Biography of a Radical.* Montreal: McGill-Queen's University Press, 1990. (Ch. 8 source 27a, in the cluster on Birmingham Political Union)
- Wakefield, C. M. *Life of Thomas Attwood.* London, 1885. (Ch. 8 source, available at Internet Archive)
- Attwood, T. *The Scotch Banker.* Birmingham: R. Wrightson, 1817. (Ch. 7 source 25)

**What I need:**
- 2–3 direct Attwood quotes from his pamphlets or speeches — particularly from the 1832 Newhall Hill rally or the 1839 Chartist petition speech. His own argument in his own voice.
- One quote from a contemporary observer (Moss has these) on what Attwood was like in person — his bearing, his speech, his manner with workers vs. with parliamentarians.
- The Disraeli "provincial banker labouring under financial monomania" quotation is already in the book; what's the fullest version of it? (The chapter currently has the short form.)

**Where it would be deployed:** Ch. 8's Birmingham Political Union sections (lines 27–55). Attwood currently has analytical voice (the chapter explains what he believed) but not personal voice (he doesn't speak in his own words at the rally that "drew perhaps 40,000 people on Newhall Hill").

## 4. Paterson (Ch. 5)

**Existing citation:** Bannister, Saxe (ed.). *The Writings of William Paterson, Founder of the Bank of England.* 3 vols. London: Effingham Wilson, 1858. (Ch. 5 source 19a)

**What I need:**
- 1–2 direct Paterson quotes from his pamphlets *other than* the disputed "out of nothing" formulation. Bannister 1858 is three volumes; the Bank of England prospectus and his Darien-promotion writings are likely the most quotable.
- Anything from contemporary observers (Pepys? Royal Society correspondence?) on what Paterson was like — his Scottish background, his "projector" temperament, his manner.
- One quote that captures his own framing of what the Bank of England was for, in his own words.

**Where it would be deployed:** Ch. 5, Paterson section. The editor persona praised this section already ("a small triumph"), but adding one or two voiced Paterson quotes would push it to NYT-cinematic level.

## 5. Backwell (Ch. 4) — *hardest, lowest priority*

**Existing citations:**
- NatWest Group Heritage Hub. Edward Backwell Archives [GB1502-EB]. (Ch. 4 source 7)
- Pepys, Samuel. *The Diary*. (Ch. 4 source 1, multiple entries on Backwell visits)
- Quinn, Stephen. (1994). *The Goldsmith-Bankers* PhD dissertation. (Ch. 4 source 4)
- Roseveare, Henry. *The Financial Revolution 1660-1760.* Longman, 1991. (Ch. 4 source 9)

**What I need:**
- The strongest 2–3 Pepys diary entries on Backwell beyond the June 17, 1662 entry already in the chapter. Pepys was a Backwell customer; there should be more observations.
- Anything from the Treasury minutes of the January 1672 emergency meeting (the "Sir Robt. Viner, Alderman Backwell, Mr. Meynell..." meeting referenced in the chapter) on what Backwell actually said.
- If the NatWest archive has any surviving Backwell letters or memoranda, even one quote.
- Quinn's dissertation may have direct material from the Backwell ledgers that gives him voice (e.g., notes in his own hand).

**Where it would be deployed:** Ch. 4's Backwell sections (currently lines ~33–37 and ~93–105). Backwell is a name attached to a balance sheet per the NYT reviewer; one or two voiced details would meaningfully change his presence.
