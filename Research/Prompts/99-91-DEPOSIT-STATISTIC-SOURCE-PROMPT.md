# Research Prompt: Source The 99.91% Deposit-Settlement Statistic

**Date requested:** 2026-04-29
**Book:** *Money, Trust, and Power*
**Chapter affected:** Chapter 8, "The Great Forgetting"
**Current manuscript handling:** The exact number has been removed from the live chapter and replaced with a softer formulation. This research is needed only if we want to restore a precise statistic or cite the softened version more confidently.

## Research Question

Find the cleanest source, denominator, and wording for the claim that, by around 1900, English/British payments by value were overwhelmingly settled through bank deposits, checks, or deposit transfers rather than Bank of England notes or cash.

The old draft formulation was:

> By 1900, 99.91 percent of transactions used deposits rather than notes.

Do not assume this wording is correct. The job is to determine what the statistic actually measured.

## What To Establish

Please answer these questions as specifically as possible:

1. What is the original source for the 99.91% / 0.09% statistic, if it exists?
2. Does it refer to England, Britain, the United Kingdom, London, or a narrower clearing-house/payment category?
3. Does it measure transaction value, transaction volume, money stock composition, bank liabilities, note circulation, clearing-house payments, or something else?
4. What year or period does it describe?
5. Is "deposits rather than notes" accurate, or should the language be "checks," "bank deposits," "deposit transfers," "bank money," "clearing-house payments," or another term?
6. Does the underlying source support an exact 99.91% claim in trade prose, or should the book retain the safer wording "less than one-tenth of one percent" / "overwhelmingly"?
7. If the exact statistic is not sourceable, what alternative published statistic would support the same historical point?
