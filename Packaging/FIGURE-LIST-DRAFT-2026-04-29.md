# Figure List Draft

**Book:** *Money, Trust, and Power*
**Purpose:** Working list for designer/typesetter. Figure specifications live in `Figures-Designer-Notes.md`.

## Recommended Core Figures

| Figure | Chapter | Title | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Chapter 1 | The Dominance of Credit Money Across 5,000 Years | Establishes that the gold standard is historically brief compared with credit-money systems. |
| 5.1 | Chapter 5 | Bank of England Money Creation: Before and After | Shows balance-sheet money creation through matched entries. |
| 5.2 | Chapter 5 | The Money-Creation Cycle, 1694 | Shows spend-first, tax-sustain mechanics. |
| 5.3 | Chapter 5 | Two-Tier Banking: Bank of England and the Country Banks | Shows hierarchy between Bank money and country-bank money. |
| 6.1 | Chapter 6 | The Global Spread of the Gold Standard, 1717-1900 | Shows London-centered adoption cascade. |
| 6.2 | Chapter 6 | The Gold Points Mechanism | Explains the operating band around parity. |
| 7.1 | Chapter 7 | Wheat Prices and General Price Level, 1790-1824 | Shows wartime inflation and post-resumption deflation. |
| 7.2 | Chapter 7 | The Deflationary Collapse, 1813-1824 | Makes the debt-deflation mechanism visible. |
| 8.1 | Chapter 8 | Notes vs. Deposits: Functionally Similar Money | Shows why the 1844 Act regulated the visible form while deposits expanded. |
| 9.1 | Chapter 9 | Gold-Standard Exit and Recovery, 1929-1937 | Shows earlier exit from gold correlating with faster recovery. |
| 11.1 | Chapter 11 | The Triffin Dilemma in Numbers | Shows gold reserves versus foreign dollar claims. |
| 12.1 | Chapter 12 | Federal Reserve Balance Sheet, 2007-2026 | Shows money creation under slack, capacity pressure, and subsequent quantitative tightening. |
| 12.2 | Chapter 12 | Money vs. Real Resources: Three Scenarios | Explains the capacity constraint. |

## Recommendation

Use a smaller final figure set if the publisher wants a leaner trade package. The highest-value figures are:

1. Figure 5.1, because it explains money creation.
2. Figure 5.2, because it explains spend-first/tax-sustain mechanics.
3. Figure 8.1, because it makes the note/deposit error visible.
4. Figure 9.1, because it dramatizes the cost of staying on gold.
5. Figure 11.1, because it makes the Triffin dilemma intuitive.
6. Figure 12.2, because it gives readers the book's final capacity metaphor.

## Open Verification Items

- Figure 8.1 should not use the old overprecise deposit statistic. The safer visual language is "bank deposits, checks, and bills dominate high-value exchange" with Pownall/Lubbock/clearing-house support.
- Figure 9.1 data should be checked against the final Eichengreen dataset or whatever source the designer uses.
- Figure 11.1 should present the Triffin figures with denominator caveats if plotted precisely.
- Figure 12.1 should use the current Fed balance-sheet endpoint if the chart extends through 2026; freeze the data immediately before submission.
