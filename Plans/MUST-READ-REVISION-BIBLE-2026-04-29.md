## North Star

The book should read as the unavoidable story of how monetary regimes choose who pays:

> Money is institutional credit, given value by the power to tax and bounded by productive capacity. Every regime that forgets this eventually rediscovers it through pain.

The must-read version is not louder. It is better organized. The reader should feel carried by the sequence:

1. Credit comes first.
2. Private trust fails without public architecture.
3. Gold works as discipline until its discipline becomes cruelty.
4. International money repeats the same problem at world scale.
5. Fiat passes some tests and fails others for reasons the history has prepared us to understand.
6. The household-budget metaphor survives because it feels moral, but the real moral question is who writes the ledger and who pays.

## Non-Negotiable Editing Rules

1. **Research before new claim.** Any added factual/statistical/current-policy claim must trace to `Research/`, chapter `sources.md`, or `Content/07-Notes-on-Sources/01-main.md`.
2. **No free-floating MMT paraphrase.** Quote or precisely cite canonical positions where the manuscript invokes Mosler, Wray, Kelton, Tcherneva, Mitchell, Bell/Kelton, Innes, Knapp, Lerner, Minsky.
3. **Steelmans stay load-bearing.** A revised paragraph may draw a stronger conclusion, but it must not make the preceding orthodox argument look decorative.
4. **Scene before mechanism when possible.** If a chapter has a human anchor, open or pivot through that human anchor before explaining the mechanism.
5. **One full explanation, later callbacks.** Once a mechanism is fully explained, later chapters should use brief callbacks unless new evidence changes the mechanism.
6. **No new "second book" in the Epilogue.** Policy clarity should be institutional and concise: what the history establishes, what it implies, what remains open.
