**Date:** 2026-04-28
**Purpose:** Single comprehensive research mission to ground all six identified gaps before chapter-level revision begins. Assumes researcher has no prior context with the book.

---

## Mission

You are doing foundational research for a Path A revision of a trade nonfiction manuscript titled *Money, Trust, and Power: Five Thousand Years of Credit, Crisis, and the Institutions That Make Money Work*. The book argues that money is institutional credit, that taxation drives currency demand, and that productive capacity (not gold, not accounting balances) is the binding constraint on what governments can spend in their own currency. The author positions this as the historical foundation under MMT's descriptive claims, while declining to advocate the full MMT policy program. The book steelmans Currency School, Friedman, Buchanan-Wagner, Sargent-Wallace, Cochrane (FTPL), and Hayek before answering them.

The revision in progress is shifting the manuscript from cautious-and-balanced to thesis-driven-and-earned. Steelmans stay intact; the author's voice surfaces in the close of Chapter 12B and a new Epilogue section. Every authorial-voice claim added in the revision must trace to demonstrable historical or analytical evidence — no free-floating MMT-canon paraphrase, no soft-handed historical claim that hasn't been checked. **The author wants to ship a thesis, not a polemic. The accuracy of the underlying research is what makes that distinction hold.**

This research mission has six parts. They are bundled because they share a delivery deadline and a citation standard, but each part must be self-contained enough to inform a specific revision phase. Read all six before you begin so you can identify cross-cutting sources.

## Author position calibration (read before drafting any answer)

The author aligns with canonical MMT (Kelton, Wray, Mosler, Tcherneva) on most descriptive claims: money as institutional credit, taxation drives currency demand, productive capacity as binding constraint, household-budget metaphor as category mistake, job guarantee as the stabilizer-based answer to public-choice concerns, eurozone as structural failure.

The author **diverges** from canonical MMT on two specific questions and your research must surface these divergences accurately:

1. **Trade and external imbalance.** Canonical MMT (especially Mosler) treats trade deficits as real benefits and external imbalances as no domestic-policy concern under floating exchange rates. The author finds this operationally correct but architecturally incomplete on global-order-stability grounds — the dollar standard's asymmetric burden on deficit countries (Triffin in modern dress) is a real problem, and Keynes's 1944 Bancor proposal — symmetric pressure on surplus and deficit countries — was the right architectural answer. The author leans **Steve Keen** on the private-debt-and-trade dynamics and **Keynes-bancor** on the international architecture question. Your job: surface where Keen and the canonical MMT position diverge on this, and where bancor-revival arguments live in the modern literature.

2. **Interest rates.** Mosler argues for permanent ZIRP. Wray and Kelton are more flexible. The author's position is more moderate still: a Fed dual mandate biased toward employment within an inflation-anchoring frame, not abandonment of the inflation-anchor altogether. Your job: surface this internal MMT spread accurately so the author's position can be calibrated as "MMT-adjacent but not Mosler" without misrepresenting any of the principals.

**Where you find canonical MMT positions, quote them directly.** Misrepresenting the canon by paraphrase is the single most damaging research error you can make for this project — the heterodox community will catch it on first reading and the book ships compromised.

## Part 1 — Trade architecture, Keen, Keynes-Bancor, and the modern revival literature

This is the highest-priority research area because it grounds the author's most distinctive divergence from canonical MMT. The book's Chapter 10 (Bretton Woods) currently treats Bancor as historical "what wasn't"; the revision will reframe it as the architectural answer to a problem that remains unsolved. The Epilogue will potentially carry a new position on trade architecture that requires defensible grounding.

**1.1 Keynes's 1944 Bancor proposal in operational detail.** Reconstruct the proposal as Keynes specified it: the international clearing union mechanism, the unit-of-account design, the symmetric pressure on surplus and deficit countries (charges on persistent surpluses as well as deficits), the asymmetric outcome at Bretton Woods, and which provisions White and the U.S. delegation specifically rejected. Primary sources: Keynes's *Collected Writings* vol. XXV–XXVI (Activities 1940–1944, "Shaping the Post-War World: The Clearing Union"); Skidelsky vol. 3 (*Fighting for Britain*); Benn Steil, *The Battle of Bretton Woods* (Princeton 2013); Eric Helleiner where he disagrees with Steil's emphasis. Quote Keynes directly on the surplus-charge mechanism. Identify what the modern literature treats as the proposal's still-live operational lessons vs. what's considered specific to 1944 conditions.

**1.2 Steve Keen's positions on trade, private debt, and where he diverges from canonical MMT.** Keen is heterodox-MMT-adjacent but not MMT canon. His distinctive contributions on trade and external balance need to be reconstructed precisely. Sources: *Debunking Economics* (2nd ed., 2011), *Can We Avoid Another Financial Crisis?* (2017), *The New Economics: A Manifesto* (2022), and his published academic papers (especially on Minsky-style financial instability and the private-debt overhang). Specific questions: What is Keen's account of the role of private debt in trade-imbalance dynamics? How does he critique the canonical MMT framing of "trade deficits are real benefits"? Where does he agree with MMT on domestic monetary sovereignty but disagree on global architecture? Has Keen written specifically on bancor-revival or international clearing union ideas? Quote where the divergence is sharpest.

**1.3 Mosler / Kelton / Wray on trade — the canonical MMT position.** Compile and quote the canonical MMT position on trade deficits, external imbalances, and dollar hegemony. Primary sources: Mosler, *The 7 Deadly Innocent Frauds of Economic Policy* (2010) and *Soft Currency Economics* (1996); Kelton, *The Deficit Myth* (2020), specifically the chapter on trade; Wray, *Modern Money Theory* (2nd ed., 2015); Tcherneva's published work on the job guarantee where it touches international questions. Quote the strongest version of "trade deficits are real benefits." Identify where the canon explicitly addresses (or declines to address) global-order-stability concerns and the asymmetric burden on deficit countries. Note where the canon treats reserve-currency status — Mosler in particular tends not to focus on it; document this directly.

**1.4 Modern bancor-revival arguments.** Compile the post-2008 literature making bancor-adjacent arguments for international monetary architecture reform. Specific sources: Joseph Stiglitz and Bruce Greenwald, *Towards a New Global Reserve System* (Stiglitz Commission 2010 and subsequent papers); Zhou Xiaochuan's March 2009 PBoC speech "Reform the International Monetary System" (full text and commentary); IMF SDR-allocation debates (especially the 2021 $650B allocation and surrounding debates); Yanis Varoufakis on a "global Plan B" where it intersects with bancor logic; Hans-Werner Sinn on TARGET2 imbalances and what they reveal about eurozone-internal-bancor analogues; Robert Triffin's original framing of the dilemma and where modern literature treats it as still-live. Output should distinguish "true bancor revival" arguments from adjacent reform proposals (e.g., SDR-as-reserve, multipolar reserve system, regional clearing).

**1.5 The synthesis.** Write a 1,000-word synthesis that the author can use directly: where canonical MMT, Keen, and the bancor-revival literature converge; where they diverge; what defensible position an author can take that is "MMT-adjacent on domestic monetary sovereignty, Keen/Keynes-bancor on global architecture." The synthesis should make the author's actual divergence from canonical MMT visible and citable.

## Part 3 — Post-1971 macro-record empirical backing

The central empirical claim of the Path A close: across the post-1971 record, advanced-economy fiat regimes have run too tight more often than too loose. Currently the manuscript asserts this; the revision needs the empirical backing in hand.

**3.1 Decade-by-decade output-gap data, 1971–2024.** Compile estimates from authoritative sources for the United States, Japan, the eurozone (or its constituent economies pre-1999), and the United Kingdom. Sources: IMF World Economic Outlook database; OECD Economic Outlook; Congressional Budget Office (potential GDP estimates for the U.S.); Bank of Japan and Cabinet Office data for Japan; ECB and European Commission for the eurozone. Where output-gap estimates are revised or contested, note the contested quality and present the range. Output a clean table: economy × decade × average output gap × number of years undershoot vs. overshoot.

**3.2 Inflation undershoot vs. overshoot frequency, 1971–2024.** For the same economies, compile decade-by-decade frequency of inflation below vs. above central bank target (where targets are explicit; inferred from policy speeches/mandates where not). Source: BIS data, FRED, ECB statistical warehouse, BoJ data.

**3.3 Specific case studies.** For each of the following, write a 500-word grounded summary the author can cite directly:
   - Japan's "lost decades" 1990–2010s as a record of binding deflationary undershoot under conventional monetary discipline. Sources: Krugman's 1998 BPEA paper, Posen's books on Japan, Bank of Japan retrospective speeches, Werner on QE origins.
   - Eurozone 2010–2019 prolonged post-crisis recession under Maastricht-style rules. Sources: Tooze *Crashed* (relevant chapters), Costas Lapavitsas on Greece, ECB speeches, OECD Economic Outlook reviews.
   - U.S. slow recovery 2009–2014 against a Federal Reserve that worried about inflation that did not arrive. Sources: Yellen's 2014 papers on labor market slack, Bernanke memoirs, Romer on insufficient stimulus.
   - U.S. 2015–2019 Yellen-era undershoot. Sources: Fed Beige Books, Yellen press conferences, Powell's framework review (2020).

**3.4 The 2021–22 episode contextualized.** Brief framing: is 2021–22 an exception or a pattern? Cross-reference with Part 4 below.

**3.5 Synthesis.** Write a 1,000-word empirical synthesis the author can cite directly: the post-1971 record establishes that advanced-economy democracies running too tight has been at least as common as running too loose, with several major cases (Japan, eurozone, slow U.S. recovery) representing extended periods of binding undershoot. Quantify where possible. Acknowledge what the data does not show.

### Citation standard (read carefully — this is the operational core of the project)

Every factual claim, statistic, quotation, and interpretive position attributed to a named thinker must be traceable to a specific primary source. Treat the citation trail as if a hostile reviewer at the *Financial Times*, *Phenomenal World*, *Journal of Economic Literature*, or *NYRB* will check every footnote. The book uses a discursive Notes-on-Sources style in the Ahamed/Tooze idiom — not endnotes — so your citations need to be specific enough that the author can convert them into either (a) an inline parenthetical attribution, (b) a discursive Notes-on-Sources paragraph that names the source and engages briefly with its place in the literature, or (c) a direct in-text quotation with full attribution.

**Format requirements by source type:**

1. **Books.** Author last name, first name. *Full Title* (italicized). Publisher, Year. Page or chapter reference for any specific claim.
   - *Example:* Mosler, Warren. *The 7 Deadly Innocent Frauds of Economic Policy*. Valance Co., 2010, ch. 4, pp. 78–84.

2. **Journal articles.** Author. "Article Title" (in quotation marks). *Journal Name* (italicized). Volume(Issue), Year, page range. DOI or URL for online articles.
   - *Example:* Sargent, Thomas, and Neil Wallace. "Some Unpleasant Monetarist Arithmetic." *Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis Quarterly Review* 5(3), Fall 1981, pp. 1–17.

3. **Working papers and institutional reports.** Author. "Title." Institution, paper series and number, Year. URL.
   - *Example:* Bernanke, Ben, and Olivier Blanchard. "What Caused the U.S. Pandemic-Era Inflation?" Hutchins Center Working Paper No. 86, Brookings Institution, June 2023.

4. **Speeches and central-bank communications.** Speaker. "Title." Venue, date. URL.
   - *Example:* Zhou Xiaochuan. "Reform the International Monetary System." People's Bank of China, 23 March 2009.

5. **Datasets.** Institution. Dataset name. Version or access date. URL.
   - *Example:* International Monetary Fund. *World Economic Outlook Database*, October 2024 edition. Accessed [date].

6. **Archival material.** Repository, collection, item identifier or folio reference, date.
   - *Example:* Bank of England Archive, Court of Directors Minutes, 26 July 1797, G4/26.

**Direct quotation rules.**

- For canonical MMT positions (Kelton, Wray, Mosler, Tcherneva, Mitchell on the claims their tradition is most identified with), **direct quotation is required, not optional.** Paraphrase carries unacceptable risk — the heterodox community will catch any paraphrase that softens or strengthens the canon, and the book ships compromised. Quote the strongest version of each position with full citation including page number.
- For steelmanned opposition (Buchanan, Wagner, Sargent, Wallace, Cochrane, Hayek, Friedman), direct quotation strongly preferred whenever the manuscript will name the position.
- Use quotation marks for inline quotes; block-quote passages longer than three lines.
- Indicate ellipses with [...] and any alterations with [bracketed text]. Never silently smooth a quotation.
- For translated material, note the translator and translation source.

**Multi-source confirmation rules.**

- Where a claim rests on multiple sources, list all of them, separated by semicolons.
- Where sources disagree, present each principal position with its own citation and explicitly identify the disagreement. Do not paper over.
- Distinguish primary sources from secondary sources. Cite primary directly where available. Skidelsky on Keynes is secondary; Keynes himself in the *Collected Writings* is primary. Steil on the Bretton Woods conference is secondary even when he is reporting from archives; the archival material itself is primary. Steil also disagrees with Helleiner on the political economy of Bretton Woods — note such disagreements where the manuscript's claim could be affected.

**Discursive note drafting.**

For each major claim or position you ground, draft a 1–3 sentence note in the Ahamed/Tooze discursive Notes-on-Sources style that the author can adapt directly into the manuscript. The note should name the source, briefly characterize its place in the literature, and signal any contested adjacent claims.

> *Example draft note:* "On Mosler's permanent-zero-interest-rate position, the operational logic is laid out most directly in *The 7 Deadly Innocent Frauds* (Valance Co., 2010, pp. 78–84), with the further argument that 'the natural rate of interest is zero' developed across his commentary at moslereconomics.com. Wray treats the question more flexibly in *Modern Money Theory* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2nd ed., 2015, pp. 232–238), allowing for inflation-responsive movement above zero in conditions where the central bank's nominal anchor is otherwise intact. Kelton's framing in *The Deficit Myth* (PublicAffairs, 2020) sits closer to Wray than to Mosler on this question and treats fiscal policy as the primary stabilization tool with monetary policy as residual."

This draft-note format is what the author needs from you for each major claim. The author will adapt your draft into the manuscript's voice; what you provide is the source-grounded substrate.

**Hyperlinks and accessibility.**

- Provide URLs for online sources (working papers, speeches, datasets, central-bank publications). Note access date for sources that may change.
- For paywalled sources, provide DOI or other persistent identifier.
- Where a source has appeared in multiple editions, cite the edition you actually consulted and note any pagination differences.

**When sources fail you.**

If a claim that this prompt or the manuscript suggests is "in the literature" turns out to be unsourceable, weaker than expected, or contested in ways the original framing didn't anticipate, **say so directly and explicitly.** Do not invent. Do not infer-and-cite. Do not stretch a marginal source into a strong one. The author would rather adjust the framing of a Path A claim than ship an indefensible citation. Flag every claim where you cannot find adequate grounding; the author will decide whether to drop the claim, soften it, or commission additional research.

**Citation density target.** Every paragraph in your synthesis sections should be traceable to at least one source. Strong claims (statistics, direct positions of named thinkers, contested historical interpretations) should carry their citations inline or in adjacent footnotes. The author's review test: can the author lift a paragraph from your synthesis, drop it into the manuscript with light editing, and immediately identify what footnote attaches to which sentence? If yes, your citation discipline is working.

**Bibliography.** End each Part with a consolidated bibliography of every source cited in that Part, formatted to the standard above. The author will use these directly as the basis for the relevant additions to the manuscript's Notes on Sources.

**Quality standards:**
- *No paraphrase of canonical MMT where direct quotation is available.* Misrepresenting Kelton, Wray, or Mosler is the single most damaging error possible for this project.
- *Distinguish settled facts from contested questions.* Where the literature is split (e.g., 2021 inflation decompositions that disagree on demand-vs-supply share), present the range and identify the principal arguments on each side.
- *Surface internal disagreements within MMT.* The author needs to position accurately as MMT-adjacent-with-specific-divergences, which requires the canon's internal spread to be visible.
- *Note what's not in the existing `Research/` folder.* The book project already has a substantial research base (~80 files) covering the historical chapters. This research is specifically additive — focused on the Path A revision's new claims. If you find your answer to a question is already adequately covered in an existing research file, note that and move on.
- *Flag where evidence is weaker than the manuscript's claim would suggest.* If the post-1971 macro record turns out to be more ambiguous than "running too tight more often than too loose," the author needs to know before the revision drafts the claim. Honest reporting is more valuable than confirmation.

**What success looks like:** the author can begin Phase 2 (Chapter 12B rewrite) and Phase 3 (Epilogue extension) of the revision plan with every authorial-voice claim grounded in a citation trail you have provided. No claim added in the revision is unsourced. The author's specific divergences from canonical MMT are positioned accurately and defensibly.
