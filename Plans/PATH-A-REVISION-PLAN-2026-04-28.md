### Calibration warning — register dial-back required

`Research Money Trust Power.md` Part 1's synthesis on trade architecture uses sharper language than the author's stated position. Specifically: it calls the dollar standard an "asymmetric, parasitic burden on the private sectors of deficit nations" and frames Bancor as "necessary surplus-charging architecture." The author's position, as stated in conversation, is milder: "fairness in trade imbalances is important for keeping a global order stable." When drafting any passage based on this Part:

- *Adopt the substance.* Keen's stock-flow-consistent argument; Bancor's symmetric pressure mechanism; Triffin's dilemma; the post-2008 revival literature.
- *Soften the register.* "Parasitic" → "asymmetric" or "structural"; "necessary surplus-charging architecture" → "designed pressure mechanism" or "symmetric adjustment design." This is the polemic-discipline rule operating on research-memo prose: substance is research-grounded and stays, language ratchets back to thesis register.

Apply the same vigilance to any other research-memo passage where the language reaches further than the author's underlying position.

## Position Calibration — Specific Author Divergences from Canonical MMT

For accuracy in Phase 2, Phase 3, and any cross-manuscript positions adopted in author's voice:

| Question | Canonical MMT (Kelton, Wray, Mosler) | Author's position | Citation trail required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money is institutional credit | Yes | Yes — aligned | Standard MMT canon |

| Job guarantee as flagship policy | Tcherneva, Wray, Mosler, Kelton — yes | Yes — aligned | Standard |
| Trade deficits are real benefits, no policy concern | Mosler, Kelton — yes | **Operationally yes; globally concerned about asymmetric burden under dollar standard.** Author leans Keen/Keynes-bancor on global-order-stability question. | Needs explicit research: where do Keen and canonical MMT diverge; what does bancor-revival literature say. |
| Permanent ZIRP | Mosler — yes; Wray/Kelton — more flexible | **Bias-toward-employment within inflation-anchoring frame; not permanent ZIRP.** | Needs explicit research on Mosler vs. Wray/Kelton on rates. |
| Dollar hegemony | Mosler/Kelton — operational sovereignty matters; reserve-currency status not a focus | **Reserve-currency dynamics matter for global-order stability; Triffin-class concerns are real.** | Needs explicit research; partial in `Dollar hegemony and the currency independence divide.md`. |

Where the author diverges from canonical MMT, the divergence must be:
1. Stated explicitly (not left implicit, which reads as misunderstanding the canon)
2. Sourced to author's preferred lineage (Keen, Keynes-bancor, Mehrling on hierarchy)
3. Calibrated as "I find X persuasive on this; canonical MMT goes further/differently for these reasons" — not as polemical disagreement with allies

This is what differentiates an honest heterodox book from a polemic. The author who agrees with allies on most things and disagrees with them publicly on specific things is doing the real work; the author who pretends total alignment is doing public relations.
